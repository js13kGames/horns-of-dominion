# Foundations
let's plan for the creation of a javascript game. It's a strategy game where you have to conquer the rainbow kingdom as an unicorn overlord. Its design is inspired by Crusader Kings by Paradox. Start Game lets you select what Unicorn realm you are the overlord of, each realm has different cities with each city having stats such as population, defensive value, economic value, standing defenses. In the cities you control you can raise armies by converting part of their population into unicorn warriors using the in game currency, which increases based on the economic value of the cities under your control. The Rainbow Kingdom has 20 cities represented as a map of interconnected nodes. In the world there are also armies posiioned in the nodes, you can move your armies to neighborging nodes. If the node is not under your control, the unit will constantly attack the node, losing warriors based on the defensive value of the node, and diminishing the standing defenses until they hit 0 and you assume control of the city. Also if there are units from the enemy you'll engage with them first, chipping away at their units until they either retreat or are defeated completely. All the factions are in place and controlled by a simple AI which will try to dominate the map (the goal of the game is to own 70% of the cities) by creating enemies and moving them into narby cities. You can also spend your currency to rebuild the defenses of a city. Big constraint for the game is, it should fit 13KB when zipped. We won't have any assets, everything should be generated and we should use a super minimalistic visual style using only emojis and flat geometry.

## Resolution
The Rainbow Kingdom now has ground under it. 12,439 B zipped of 13,312 (873 B headroom).

How it's done — src/terrain.js bakes the whole backdrop once per map into an offscreen canvas, so the live frame pays a single drawImage and detail costs nothing:

- The coastline is the map's own convex spread. For 30 angles it takes the support function of that seed's 20 city positions, pads the axis dips, adds a smoothed random margin, and draws it as one quadratic curve. Every city is inside by construction, and every seed gets a different silhouette.
- The rainbow uses the five realm colours — already ROYGB-ish, so no new palette — arcing at radius 760 through the sky above the island, each band stroked with a colour→transparent gradient so its legs fade out instead of ending hard.
- Mountains and lakes are rejection-sampled into ground no road or city is using, with clearance scaled to each feature's own size.
- Lakes shimmer on the live layer, capped at 0.15 alpha so water can never compete with a red siege pulse.
- The board zooms out to 0.72 so there's sky on every side and room for the underside to taper into the void.

## Resolution

Loyalty is in and the game still resolves. 13,216 B of 13,312 — 96 B headroom, so it fits, just.

The mechanic. Each city carries a 5-slot ledger summing to 100 plus a native realm from the draft. One helper moves points onto a faction proportionally from the others, so the ledger never needs renormalising. Per tick: whichever host stands in the city pulls loyalty toward its owner, the native realm pulls back, a city over 80% loyal to its holder becomes native to them, and an occupier under 20% gets a rising. The rebel host is flagged rb, which zeroes the walls the moment it acts — it reuses the whole existing capture path, so it walks in without a siege but still fights anything garrisoned there first. canRaise gained the 60% gate, which the AI inherits automatically since it calls the same function.

Two things I had to add beyond your spec, both because the total-conquest victory stopped being reachable:

- Assimilation. Without it a conquered city drifts back to native forever and must be garrisoned permanently. The first run gave 4 stalemates in 200 and a 92,000-tick tail.
- The native pull scales with how much of the map the native realm still holds. This one was a genuine feedback loop: a rising hands a dead realm an army, the army keeps it flagged alive, so its cities keep rising for it, forever. Scaling by min(1, cnt(native)/3) means a swept-away realm stops rallying anyone, and conquest accelerates as you win.

Both are single tunables if you want them off.

Balance. 400 games across two independent seed ranges: zero stalemates, p50 ~4,345 ticks, nothing over 24k. Wins stay flat across the five realms. Games are genuinely longer than before — p90 went 5,950 → ~7,400 and p99 9,427 → ~13,000 — which is the honest cost of having to hold what you take.

The ladder compressed to 0/24/36/46 because loyalty taxes fast expanders hardest, same as occupation did in round 6. Gold couldn't fix it — under loyalty the bottleneck is holding ground, not economy, so I added a per-rung pacification multiplier. That's now the dominant lever (my first guess overshot to 63/77) and the ladder is back at 0 / 24 / 44 / 63.

cmd-test gained eight assertions covering the ledger, the drift, the garrison outpulling it, both sides of the raise gate, and a rising taking a city with its walls still at 65.

## Resolution

Round 14 is done. The five-value loyalty ledger is gone; each city now carries na (native realm) and one number u.

The system (src/sim.js step 5c, T in state.js)

- u starts at 0 and is 0 whenever na holds the city.
- Any other realm taking it sets u = max(u, 70) — a second captor never talks it down.
- Checks run every T.slow = 10 ticks, the only non-per-tick step in tick(). Per check a conquered city gathers T.stir, and the occupier subtracts T.pace scaled by min(1, warriors / (pop × T.hold)) — the same garrison calculation as before, so a token warrior still pacifies nobody.
