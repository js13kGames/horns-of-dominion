# Round 4

## Topics
1. It's easy to move unit by mistake when a unit is selected and I want to examine a city, and I have to antinaturally click "nothing" to deselect a unit and be able to select a city; I think we need an action to "Mobilize" from the warband panel when it's selected, which then requires selecting a city or canceling? It's either this or left click to select, right click to move, which loses us mobile.
2. The ending was anticlimatic, I think an ending by total conquest is more satisfactory and should be doable.
3. Is it possible to add difficulty levels for the AI? seems too easy right now.

## Resolution

1. Clicking never moves anything. A map click now means exactly one thing: select. Marching goes through 🎯 Mobilize on the warband panel, which arms targeting — the next city click is the destination, every city rings faintly to show the whole map is fair game, and Cancel or Esc backs out (Esc cancels targeting first, clears the selection on a second press). The node-behind-click for retreating went away with it, replaced by a proper ↩ Turn back button that shows the flee cost when you're engaged — strictly more discoverable, since nothing ever told you that trick existed. The DOM test now asserts the actual bug is fixed: clicking a city with a warband selected does not move it.

2. Total conquest. All 20 cities. As the plan warned, this is where the pacing risk was, and it landed: p99 went to 69k ticks and one game hit 116k. Three forcing functions now close it out — a realm down to its last two cities sheds walls and starves its hosts (the turtling stack was the real blocker, not the walls), and fortification upkeep decays as a war drags on. The end screen also reports the campaign now: cities held, taken, lost, hosts broken, largest host, duration, rung.

3. Four difficulty rungs — Dreamer / Duelist / Warlord (default) / Tyrant, scaling AI income, decisions per turn, and army ceiling. Your side is untouched. diff-test.mjs pits one rung against four Duelists over 150 games each:

Dreamer   0.5  1   0.5     0.0%
Duelist   1    1   1      19.0%     ← today's AI, and a fair share is 20%
Warlord   1.4  2   1.3    47.0%     ← new default
Tyrant    2    3   1.7    66.0%

Duelist landing at 19% against an expected 20% is the check that the neutral rung really is neutral. The default is now roughly 2.4× the AI you found too easy, with Tyrant above that.

Three bugs the measurements caught, all mine:

- Gating the slow rung on slot parity looked fine but the slot→realm rotation meant some realms would get three times as many even-numbered turns as others. Replaced with a per-realm toggle.
- My first Dreamer design (half the decisions) made all-Dreamer games stall — 28 stalemates in 400 — because nobody drove the war. Rebuilt as economically crippled but thinking at normal speed: 1 stalemate in 150, and still the weakest rung.
- +process.argv[3] || 2 in the harness meant rung 0 silently ran as rung 2. Identical statistics to the digit gave it away.

State now — 1,200 games at the default rung: zero stalemates, p50 3,610 ticks (30 min at 1×, 3.8 min at 8×), p99 ~10,000, 0.3% running long. Win rates flat across the realms. All six harnesses pass, including 45 DOM assertions. 10,104 B zipped of 13,312, 3,208 B headroom.

Worth knowing: total conquest at Warlord actually finishes faster than 70% did against the old AI — a stronger AI plus the crumbling-realm rules make wars decisive rather than long. The thing I can't judge is whether Mobilize feels like one click too many in practice; that's the call to make while playing.

## Topics

2. Nowhere is your faction noted, Add in the top left corner
4. Only show "current work" (i.e. the progress ring) for cities you control
3. Fog of War

- If a path is not adjacent to a city you control, it's hidden by the fog of war: show it grayed out.
- Scouting units. A warband fighting on an enemy city removes that city and its connected roads from the fog of war. 
- Scouting units. A warband traveling a road removes that road from the fog of war. 
- Only show enemy warbands that are in roads or cities not covered by the fog of war.

## Resolution

1. Mobilize is automatic. Clicking one of your own warbands arms targeting immediately — the next city click is the destination. Worth flagging: this is the behaviour that caused your round-four complaint. What's different now is that the mode is visible (every city rings, the panel says "choose a destination") and cancellable via ✖ Cancel or Esc. If it still bites, it's a one-line revert.

2. Your banner now opens the HUD, top-left, in your realm's colour.

3. Fog of war. Your answer on transient reveals turned out to simplify things considerably: because the fog closes behind a host, there's nothing to store — no discovered-city flags, no visited-road set, nothing to reset between games. It's three pure functions over the current board in src/sim.js. Roads and city positions always draw, just greyed; the banner and every number behind it go with the fog, so scouting has a point. A fogged city also shows 🏰 rather than 👑 — the crown would have leaked capital status, which is exactly the intel the fog is meant to hide.

4. Progress rings now render only on cities you control.

The verification worth mentioning: since fog is purely derived, the simulation should be untouched — so sim-test and diff-test had to come back identical to the digit. They did: p50 3620 / p90 5635 / p99 10557 / max 78794, winners 77/71/79/83/90, ladder 0.0 / 19.0 / 47.0 / 66.0. That's stronger evidence than any assertion I could write that fog didn't leak into the balance.

dom-test.mjs gained seven fog assertions, including the live-reveal behaviour you specified — plant a host on a distant enemy city and its panel opens up; withdraw it and the fog closes again. All six harnesses pass. 10,351 B zipped of 13,312, 2,961 B headroom.

Two things you should know:

- The AI plays with full information. The fog is a player-facing layer only. Making it mutual would mean rewriting the AI's scoring to work from partial knowledge, which is a much larger job than this round and would change every balance number I've measured.
- The dev server you're looking at is the one from earlier, not a new one. My npm run dev hit EADDRINUSE and exited because the previous instance still held port 8080 — but that instance watches src/, so it rebuilt on every save. I checked the served page carries the fog code and its build timestamp matches the source mtimes exactly, so what you're seeing is current.

The muster-ring restriction is render-only and isn't observable through the test stub, so that one is verified by reading the code rather than by assertion — worth a glance when you play.

All five are in — reload http://localhost:8080 (the watching dev server has rebuilt).

1. Fog now hides events, not just terrain. Clash markers, capture flashes and log lines are all gated on line of sight at the moment they happen. A battle in the dark produces nothing: no ⚔️, no shockwave, no "host is shattered", no "X falls from Y". The DOM test asserts both halves — a fight at a fogged city draws nothing and never reaches the log, while the same fight at a visible one does.

2. Attack notifications. A red toast across the top plus a log line when one of your cities comes under attack or one of your warbands is caught in a fight. Edge-triggered with a per-entity flag, so you get told once per attack rather than every tick.

3. Cities under attack pulse — a red ring breathing at ~1Hz, on any visible city with enemies at its gates. Fogged cities don't pulse, for the same reason they don't flash.

4. Sacking hurts. A captured city keeps 45% of its populace (was 75%) and is left cowed for about three musters — the Raise button shows the countdown and refuses. This is the one item with balance consequences, so I measured it.

5. Cities-held removed from the end screen.

What the measurement turned up. The occupation lockout falls hardest on whoever expands fastest, which quietly compressed the difficulty ladder — Warlord dropped 47%→35%, Tyrant 66%→59%. That's a regression against a ladder I'd measured and reported, so I nudged the two upper rungs' economy and army-cap knobs once to restore it: 0 / 19 / 40 / 61, monotonic and well spread. Pacing across 1,200 games at the default rung: zero stalemates, p50 ~3,900 ticks (33 min at 1×, 4 min at 8×), p99 ~10,500, worst case 24k.

Two silent failures worth naming, both in my own tooling:

- The "Cowed" button label never actually landed — my scripted replacement didn't match the file and reported nothing. The DOM test caught it, and I re-applied it with a tool that errors on a miss instead of failing quietly. Same class of bug as the harness tick-cap and the falsy-zero rung from earlier rounds; I should stop using silent string replacement for edits like these.
- dist-test.mjs crashed on the new #toast element because its stub only knew the five original element ids. That was a stub gap, not a product bug — the real page has the div — but it meant the shipped-artifact gate was failing while &&-chaining hid the exit code from me. The stub now creates elements on demand so it can't drift from index.html again.

All six harnesses pass. 10,730 B zipped of 13,312, 2,582 B headroom.
